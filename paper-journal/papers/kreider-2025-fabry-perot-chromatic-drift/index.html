What this note follows

The published article is titled “Quantification of broadband chromatic drifts in Fabry–Pérot resonators for exoplanet science.” The accessible preprint uses “Quantifying.” This note uses the preprint for the technical reading and the publisher record for the 2025 bibliographic details; the two versions are not silently conflated.

Source findings

What the authors report

Kreider et al. compare Fabry–Pérot modes with laser frequency combs in HPF and NEID, tracking up to about 13,000 modes. Wavelength-dependent drift is modelled through changes in dielectric mirror layers in addition to a bulk cavity term. The preprint reports frequency-drift precision approaching 10⁻¹¹ per day, equivalent to about 3 mm/s per day. That is a precision on drift behaviour—not a demonstrated 3 mm/s on-sky stellar RV floor. Interpretation and technical reasoning

The resonance includes the mirrors

A useful schematic resonance condition is 2knL + φ₁ + φ₂ = 2πm, where k is vacuum wavenumber and φ₁, φ₂ are reflection phases. If the reflection phases vary with wavelength and change with time, the mode grid need not drift as a rigid scale. This simplified expression explains why a cavity-length-only model can be incomplete.

The distinction is between stabilising a physical assembly and stabilising every optical resonance it produces. A sensor or reference that strongly constrains one degree of freedom may leave another unobserved. A lock at one wavelength therefore needs broadband validation before its correction is extrapolated.

Interpretation and technical reasoning

Separating the ruler from the instrument

A calibrator is measured through the same instrument whose drift it is meant to diagnose. That creates an identifiability problem: detector motion, illumination changes and a moving reference can produce superficially similar residuals. An independent frequency reference helps break that ambiguity, but only over the region and cadence actually sampled.

I would retain wavelength-resolved residual maps throughout the analysis. A weighted mean is useful for operational monitoring, yet opposite-sign errors in different spectral regions may cancel in that mean while still biasing a star with different line weights.

Interpretation and technical reasoning

Why a physical fit still needs validation

A plausible coating model need not uniquely identify which microscopic material process occurred. Thickness and refractive-index perturbations can have correlated signatures. I would report the model’s explanatory power separately from any claim of a uniquely recovered physical mechanism.

Extrapolation is another boundary. A fit over one time interval is not automatically a forecast through maintenance, a temperature cycle or a change in illumination. The reference cadence should be justified by prediction error, not simply by an attractive average drift rate.

Proposed work · not performed

How I would test an FP calibration strategy

Wavelength holdout: fit on separated wavelength regions and predict modes not used in the fit. Compare a bulk-only model with a modest chromatic model under the same validation scheme.

Time holdout: forecast the next reference interval and record the full residual distribution. Refit only after evaluating that forecast; otherwise model flexibility can disguise poor predictive performance.

Science weighting: propagate the residual map through representative stellar line weights. A broadband mean and a cool-star RV estimator need not have the same sensitivity to chromatic error.

Interpretation and technical reasoning

A practical calibration lesson

For an EXOhSPEC-style stability budget, I would keep calibrator ageing separate from the spectrograph’s thermal response. The two can share timescales while requiring different interventions.

The operational product should include an evolving wavelength-dependent calibration residual and its uncertainty. That is more informative than a single reassuring offset, and gives a concrete criterion for when an independent absolute reference is needed.

Extended reading · methods, assumptions and interpretation

Following the full technical argument

The central inference is differential and broadband

The experiment does not treat the spectrograph as an immutable detector of an etalon. It compares etalon resonances against a frequency-comb reference across the band. Changes common to both can be distinguished from changes of the etalon relative to that reference. The spectral coverage is crucial because one reference wavelength cannot reveal how distant modes separate.

For a simple cavity, increasing optical length lowers the resonant frequency at fixed mode number. This yields a comparatively simple fractional-frequency pattern. Once the mirrors contribute a wavelength-dependent reflection phase, the same intuitive rigid-ruler picture is no longer complete. Opposite trends at different wavelengths need not mean the physical spacer simultaneously grows and shrinks.

That distinction also sets an operational requirement. A densely populated calibrator can provide excellent interpolation while still carrying an evolving reference error. The density of its lines and their absolute long-term stability are independent properties. Both must appear in a calibration budget.

Why coating structure is an explanatory model, not just a flexible curve

A multilayer mirror is designed through interference among reflected amplitudes. Its reflectivity and reflection phase depend on layer thicknesses and refractive indices. Small material changes can therefore reshape the frequency response even when a mechanical spacer is extremely stable.

The paper’s perturbative comparison asks which layer changes produce the observed wavelength pattern. This is more constrained than fitting an arbitrary polynomial to drift. The shape, sign changes and relative spectral behaviour become evidence about where a perturbation could reside. A successful model should explain those features rather than merely reproduce the mean rate.

However, fitting thickness and refractive-index changes does not uniquely identify their microscopic cause. Stress relaxation and a surface alteration can have related effective optical consequences. The uncertainty in the manufactured coating structure also matters. I would separate three claims: chromatic drift is observed; certain coating perturbations explain its structure; the exact underlying ageing mechanism still needs dedicated material evidence.

How to read alternatives, residuals and long-baseline implications

A competing mechanism should be compared in shape as well as amplitude. A temperature or alignment perturbation large enough to match the overall scale can still predict the wrong wavelength dependence. The alternative-mechanism figure is therefore part of the argument, not an incidental appendix.

Residuals identify what the physical model has not explained. Smooth structure may indicate an incomplete layer model or imperfect knowledge of the as-built coating. Treating every residual as independent noise would exaggerate confidence in extrapolating the correction to new times or wavelengths.

For a survey, the practical question is how accurately an etalon can predict its own future grid between independent reference observations. That requires held-out temporal validation and science-weighted residuals. This paper motivates a better calibration architecture, but it does not remove stellar variability, detector effects or the need for an end-to-end RV error budget.
